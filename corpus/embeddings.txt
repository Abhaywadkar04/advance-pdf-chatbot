Embeddings are numerical vector representations of text (or images, audio, etc.) produced by a machine learning model. Each piece of text — a word, sentence, or chunk — is converted into a fixed-length list of floating point numbers, for example 768 numbers long.

The key property of embeddings is that texts with similar meaning are placed close together in this high-dimensional vector space, while texts with unrelated meaning are placed far apart. This closeness is typically measured using a similarity metric such as cosine similarity or Euclidean distance.

Because embeddings capture meaning rather than exact wording, they allow a computer to find semantically related information even when the query uses completely different words than the source text. For example, a query about "how to fix a flat tire" could retrieve a document about "repairing a punctured wheel" because their embeddings are close in vector space, even though they share almost no exact words.

Embeddings are generated by embedding models, which are neural networks trained specifically to place semantically similar inputs near each other. Popular embedding models include Google's Gemini embedding models, OpenAI's text-embedding models, and open-source models such as those from the Sentence-Transformers library.

Embeddings are the foundation of semantic search and vector databases: text is embedded once at indexing time, stored, and then at query time the user's question is embedded and compared against the stored vectors to find the closest matches.
